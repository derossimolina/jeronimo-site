**Jerônimo de Rossi Molina** reads government decisions by what they cost: the tax that changes, the contract that stalls, the budget that slips. He is a political risk analyst, a business consultant and a PhD candidate in Administration at the University of Caxias do Sul. And he says which side he is on.

> Academic in method, consultant on deadlines, pro-market in position. All three in plain sight.

## Three roles, one yardstick

Researchers, consultants and columnists usually hide at least one of these roles. Jerônimo publishes all three, because each one holds the others to account.

- **Researcher.** His research line is political risk, anticipation and technological dependence in public administration. Academia supplies the method: a clear question, a traceable source and a conclusion that survives the counter-test.
- **Consultant.** He analyses political, geopolitical and economic risk for companies. Clients buy the reading. They do not buy the conclusion or the agenda.
- **Columnist.** In the [newsletter](/en/newsletter/), a position on the week's topic. In the [Analyses](/en/analises/), the fact sheet: sourced and dated facts, a probability range, a horizon and what to watch (in Portuguese).

The academic title is not a seal. What he publishes stands on documents and data, not on authority.

## The declared side

Jerônimo does not sell neutrality. He says where he stands and hands over the document for anyone to check.

The position is **pro-market**, within a **liberal-progressive** lens: a smaller and predictable state, open markets, individual liberties and equality before the law. It is not cheerleading or campaigning, and the agenda belongs to neither pole.

Every piece asks the question of those who produce, invest, hire and answer for decisions under uncertain rules: what changes in cost, time, tax, contract, predictability and regulatory risk.

## The same yardstick for everyone

Criticism of the federal government is a coverage rule, not an exception. Spending, taxes, regulation, state companies, public credit, foreign policy and relations with Congress are measured against the government's own discourse and against results.

Every criticism has four parts: **the act, the date, the rule or the number, and the effect**. No attributed intent, no backroom theories, no adjective in place of data.

The opposition, the other branches and state and municipal governments go through the same test. Criticising the government is not an endorsement of those who oppose it, and getting one point right does not absolve the rest. Rumours, chain messages, fraud claims without proof and hidden motives are not used as sources or as rhetorical questions.

The tone is combative against acts and ideas, never against people. The voice has three demands: **direct** (the conclusion is in the first line), **dry** (short sentences; when we don't know, we write "we don't know") and **credible** (whoever disagrees checks the document and finds no factual error).

## How he reports

1. **Fact with source.** Every relevant piece of information points to a document, official data or a traceable source, with a date.
2. **No document, no claim.** What has no document goes in as a question or stays out. An anonymous source without independent confirmation is not enough.
3. **Data before adjectives.** Numbers, series and public documents whenever possible.
4. **Uncertainty as a range.** Scenarios come with a probability range and a horizon, never a single number.
5. **Fast correction.** Factual errors are corrected within 24 hours and flagged to readers.

## Consulting and editorial separation

Consulting uses the same method and the same open sources as the published analysis. Clients are not named, and these rules apply:

1. **The agenda is not the client's.** Clients do not choose, review or veto editorial pieces, and get no favourable coverage.
2. **Conclusions are not for hire.** The conclusion does not change with who pays, and no deliverable contradicts what was published about the same fact.
3. **Client material never becomes a piece.** Client information, requests and identity are confidential.
4. **Declared ties.** When a piece covers a topic involving an active client, the tie is disclosed in the piece itself.

The brand does no lobbying, does not broker access to public officials, does not run election campaigns, issues no legal opinions and makes no investment recommendations.

## Who pays

The brand is funded by **subscribers and advertisers**. If another source of revenue appears, it will be listed on this page.

- **Identified sponsorship.** Every sponsored insertion is marked as advertising and kept apart from editorial content.
- **Free agenda.** Sponsors do not choose, review or veto editorial content, nor get favourable treatment in coverage.
- **Conflict of interest.** There is no sponsorship from anyone under ongoing critical coverage without the tie being disclosed to readers.

## Party membership

The editor has been **a member of the Podemos party since May 2026**.

We say so here and in the piece itself when it deals with the party or its direct opponents. Membership does not ground the analysis: the position comes from the lens and the document, not from the party label. The party may change sides; the editorial line does not change with it.

If a piece's conclusion coincides with the party's position, we check the coincidence against the source before publishing. We do not declare support for candidates or parties in our pieces.
